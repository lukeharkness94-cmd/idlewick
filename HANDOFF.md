## Build and delivery

- Edit [index.html](index.html), [js/](js/), [css/](css/) and [fonts/](fonts/) as appropriate. The script order in index.html is part of the build: feature modules extend `window.DQ` in place, and later UI or art modules wrap the earlier implementations. Trace the final wrapper before changing a shared route, renderer or action.
- `python3 scripts/preview.py` serves the rebuilt standalone build at http://127.0.0.1:8765/. Windows uses `py`. Use `--host 0.0.0.0` when a cloud preview requires it.
- `python3 scripts/package-game.py` regenerates [Play Idlewick.html](<Play Idlewick.html>) for browser and offline use. Commit the generated file with playable source changes. Browser preview starts a save for its own origin or profile, so use explicit export/import when moving progress.
- Keep changes small and use the repository workflow in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Main is the delivery branch. Run the full suite on the actual merge candidate, with isolated test saves.

## Current player experience

**Shell and activity pages.** Keep the feel of an illustrated pixel RPG. Desktop has a top page menu and a 180px rail with Now, Combat, then the skill list. The rail fits its rows to the viewport and keeps skill ordering stable. Phone navigation keeps every destination reachable. Main actions stay in view on desktop while long catalogues or lists scroll inside their panels. Keep artwork, names and requirements legible at laptop widths, on ultrawide displays and in both themes. Use visible tabs and illustrated choices throughout. Reveal the current task and its next useful action before secondary numbers and advice. [Hearthside Journal](docs/hearthside-journal.md) and the [spacing audit](docs/menu-spacing-audit.md) describe the visual and interaction patterns.

**Progression, sources and shop.** Judge reachability from a fresh hero using actual supplies and actions. Unlocks should describe the real requirement and lead to the exact objective. An item-source link selects the reachable source without starting it, preserves a return route, and offers explicit travel when it is required. Do not reveal an undiscovered item through a tooltip, route label or recipe image. Discoverable recipes have their own shelf. Keep item comparisons grounded in the equipped item and show a unique effect near the top. Tool tiers and workshop ranks show concrete improvements, required materials and preceding tiers through the existing shop and smithing systems. Equipment swaps and claims conserve inventory and earned progress. See [base-game equipment](docs/base-game-equipment.md), [upgrade purpose](docs/upgrade-purpose-audit.md) and [connected skills](docs/cross-skill-update.md).

**Quests, narrative and restoration.** Quests use a chapter list and a stable reading card with objective counts, status and a clear Accept quest action. Continue and the pinned action choose the same unfinished objective. Claim rewards in the existing card without rebuilding the list. Main-quest conversations use a single stage with the speaker and player-paced dialogue. Keep character voices, useful story consequences, and journal replays that never pay out again. Projects show actual scene changes, benefits and acknowledgements. Local standing is visible for the village and is earned through restoration. See [quest journal](docs/quest-journal.md), [quest accessibility](docs/quest-accessibility.md) and [story and restoration](docs/story-and-restoration.md).

**Combat.** Your fighter contains Equipped, Fighting style and Abilities. The centre is Where to fight or the active stage. Under it, show the current foe or the available challenges beside Food. Preserve the shared equipment slots and exact gear picker. Ordinary areas use weighted encounters, and tracking chooses a specific target. Explore instead releases that target into the same area's weighted loop. The arena reads the current fight and stays still as opponents change. Delay hit, defeat and replacement feedback until impact, keeping motion refresh-rate independent and reduced-motion friendly.

- Melee, Ranged and Magic are usable styles from the start. Melee and Ranged have Techniques or Shots plus a practice yard. Magic keeps Spellbook, Runes and Alchemy, including through the phone combat navigation. Practice requires the matching weapon, archery practice uses no arrows, and practice awards style XP without enemy kills or loot. Fight entry explains missing arrows or runes and points to supplies. Now opens the exact running task. The legacy `#arts` route opens the equipped style's book.
- Combat still owns hostile encounters and bosses. Hunting owns quarry. Before Hunting is learned, an active quest can offer the exact animal fight. After it is learned, the quest routes through Hunting with its existing quest exemptions. Early Cowhide points to a reachable combat source. See [combat balance](docs/combat-balance.md) and [first hour](docs/first-hour.md) for the recorded evidence and its assumptions.

**Hunting, Crafting and Beastmastery.** Fieldwork uses the existing repeating `train:lure:<town>` action. Crafted field traps use the tier and stock rules, fieldwork awards Hunting XP, and the existing material pools and egg hooks apply. Hunting ground has 200 quarry across eight grounds and five tiers. Grounds are visible from any town, with road gates, kit and bait requirements where applicable. The quarry cycle tracks, sets a trap and then strikes, with a chance of escape. A catch counts for relevant quests, Guide and loot without granting combat XP. Companion expeditions, eggs, ranch care and bonds belong to Beastmastery. Preserve existing companion and quarry IDs, deadline rules and the two quarry aliases `wild_goat` → `hill_goat` and `wild_turkey` → `turkey` in saved activity, queue and Guide data. [Hunting and trap design](docs/hunting-crafting-loop.md) · [Beastmastery](docs/beastmastery.md)

**Other persistent systems.** The Field guide tracks creatures, resources and repeatable activities. Collection shows actual progress and recorded odds. World Life adds optional events, discoveries, museum entries and other pursuits through the existing save. Farming has distinct crops and livestock workspaces. Scholarship groups related tiers. Workers use finite supply jobs and stop wages on completion. Use the established systems and see [connected systems](docs/connected-systems.md), [mastery guilds](docs/mastery-guilds.md) and [skill connections](docs/skill-connections-review.md) when extending them.

**Art, sound and notifications.** Use the existing original code-drawn pixel art and lettering variables. Sprites render on whole pixels, with distinct silhouettes and palettes for items and creatures. Motion uses elapsed time or native browser animations. Preserve reduced motion, accessible button labels, keyboard and touch access to secondary detail, and readable light and dark themes. Short first-use skill lessons appear once and remain available in Help. Keep routine events quiet, make unlocks clear, and keep repetitive task effects independent from music and village ambience. [Pixel-art style](docs/pixel-art-style.md) · [Writing review](docs/writing-review.md)

## Saves and release status

The schema is version 2. Browser storage uses `dashboard-quest-save-v2` and `dashboard-quest-backups`. Use Settings → Saves & backups to export/import a save string or download/load a backup file. Player progress does not follow a GitHub checkout. Keep fixtures isolated, never commit personal saves, and verify load and resume after migration.

Offline progress runs at full speed for the first two hours and half speed for the next six, for up to five effective hours. Work, events and rewards that depend on the game clock should remain consistent in the live and offline paths. Absolute-time companion deadlines use their existing clock and must resolve once.

The intended Steam targets are Windows x64 and macOS universal. The desktop bridge maps the save keys to `hero.json` and `backups.json` under the active profile. The App ID is unset. Signing, target-platform validation, depot delivery and cross-device Auto-Cloud remain open. Use the [desktop release guide](desktop/STEAM.md) for the contract and release checklist.
