# Idlewick working instructions

Read HANDOFF.md for current product decisions and architecture before changing the game.

- `main` is the sole working and delivery branch. The redundant `work` branch was removed after verifying identical history. Pull from and push to `main`; do not recreate `work`.

- The source is index.html, js/ and css/. Play Idlewick.html is generated; rebuild it with `python3 scripts/package-game.py` after changes.
- Run `node tests/regression.cjs` for engine, persistence, notifications, project pins and onboarding. The test uses only Node built-ins.
- Preview with `python3 scripts/preview.py`. Use `--host 0.0.0.0` only when the cloud preview needs it. Windows uses `py` instead of `python3`.
- Preserve the illustrated task cards and large visible action target. The user explicitly rejected replacing the game with dropdown selectors. Keep the tree and logs, ore-bearing rocks, crafting output and actual enemy visible.
- No dropdowns or collapsed disclosures anywhere. Use visible wrapping buttons, tabs and illustrated cards.
- Design size is 1920 × 1080. On desktop (> 860px) `js/scale.js` zooms the page by innerWidth / 1920, capped at innerHeight / 1080 when that is larger than 1 (ultrawide screens gain width, not giant text; `html.lw-wide` over 2300 page pixels), times Settings → Interface size (90/100/115%), and never below 85% (MIN_ZOOM): wider screens scale up, laptops (1280–1600) stop at 85% and the layout adapts (`html.lw-1600` / `html.lw-1300` for page widths under 1600 / 1300) instead of shrinking text further. Readability floor: no visible text under 13px in the design (so ≥ 11 screen px at 85%); `tests/ui-fit.cjs` checks this at 1280 × 720. CSS desktop breakpoints between 861 and 1920 were collapsed to 860/861; use `--vh`/`--vh100`/`--vw100` instead of vh/vw, `html.short` for scaled heights under 940px, and `DQ.toPage()` to convert screen coordinates (getBoundingClientRect, mouse) into page pixels. Phones (≤ 860px) stay responsive at 100%.
- Desktop shell (one-screen overhaul, approved concept): the page menu (Home, Quests, World, Village, Hero, Shop) lives in the top bar (`#topnav`, `js/shell.js`); the 208px left column holds the Now card (current task, Stop/Retreat), Combat and every skill. Phones keep the bottom bar and the task strip in the top bar. Do not move page links back into the left column.
- Keep the 208px desktop rail. Skill rows show icon, name, level or lock and one progress bar (44px, consistent padding); exact XP lives in the row's hover/focus detail, so every skill fits at 1440 × 900 without crushing rows. On windows shorter than 1180 page pixels (`html.rail-tight`, with 17 skills) rows tighten to 36px and the Now card to two lines so the column never scrolls down to 720px tall; skill emblems stay 32px (whole-number pixel scale). Never shrink text to fit within the 1920 design.
- Choice cards stack: picture on top (64px art box), then name, one short caption and state, centred. Locked cards show the blacked-out picture, ??? and a short requirement (e.g. "Lv 26 · Reach Saltmere"); the full wording lives in the tooltip and beside the scene when selected. Do not put text in a narrow column beside the art.
- On desktop activity pages (gather, make, farm) the page never scrolls: the action panel stays still and only the list of choices scrolls inside its panel. Every other desktop page also locks to the window (the content area scrolls only as a last resort): Quests is a scrolling list of rows beside the selected quest in full; Scholarship, Exploration, Shop, Map and Skill levels scroll only their long list; Combat (three columns at 1920) keeps the fight, the selected creature, a one-row fighting-style strip (weapon + Accurate/Aggressive/Defensive or Offensive/Defensive) and the creature/area list on screen; your fighter, abilities and food scroll beneath the fight (`.combat-col`); the creature list and preparation/boss/loot (`.combat-side`) scroll in their own columns. Combat shows only the fight, the fighting-style strip and the creatures to fight (`js/combat_simple.js`); combat skills, abilities, food, creature preparation, the boss and loot open as live pop-ups from the `.combat-tools` button row, and Equipment goes straight to the Inventory page. Each button shows its state (danger, gear worn, food health and auto-eat, abilities filled, combat level, boss readiness) with a warning tone where attention is needed, so most checks need no click. Keep obvious text minimal; do not put that detail back on the page. House: cottage, bonuses and room buttons on the left; the chosen room's furniture as cards on the right. Page tabs, the "Back to …" link and pinned quests share one `#pagebar` row. `node tests/ui-fit.cjs` enforces this.
- Backdrops (`js/backdrop.js`): code-drawn pixel scenes behind the game under a theme wash, with slightly see-through panels. Outside combat the player picks Starlit hills (default) or Twilight forest in Settings; Combat shows a scene for the area being fought or previewed (two per region, keyed by area id such as `saltmere:1`). Add a recipe there for any new area.
- Quest givers (`js/folk.js`): every giver has a code-drawn 32 × 32 portrait (shown on quest rows, the quest card and the People tab beside Quests) and short notes revealed by finishing their quests ("meet" once any of their quests is under way). The People page shows only what is known: no counts, no hints, no "do X to unlock". New notes arrive as a quiet bell line. Any new giver needs an entry with a look and notes.
- Type standard (Nov 2026 brief): 16.5px body, 16px button labels, 14px meta text. Lettering (`js/lettering.js`, Settings → Lettering) defaults to Storybook: Cinzel headings, Alegreya book serif for text, buttons and numbers (weight 500, lining figures); Adventurer (MedievalSharp headings), Pixel (Silkscreen + Pixelify Sans) and Original (Alegreya SC + Alegreya Sans) are the alternatives. Use the --f-display/--f-body/--f-ui/--f-mono variables, never a hard-coded family. Silkscreen (local, `fonts/`) is the pixel display face for big numbers and short headlines only (e.g. the welcome-back summary); never for body text. See the "UI standard" block in css/style.css.
- Each item and resource must remain visually distinct; do not restore shared tool sprites, fish recolours, identical tree silhouettes or metal artwork for leather equipment. `node tests/art-pixel.cjs` enforces full coverage, pixel-unique items and distinct fish and tree silhouettes.
- The art direction is original pixel art drawn in code: `js/pixelart.js` (drawing system, palette, hero), `js/pixel_items.js`, `js/pixel_creatures.js`, `js/pixel_world.js` (trees, rocks, scenes, restoration, furniture, icons) and `js/pixel_runtime.js` (routes every art hook; installed last). Follow `docs/pixel-art-style.md`: top-left light, five-step hue-shifted ramps from the shared palette, selective coloured outlines, whole-number scales with pixelated rendering. No image files, no AI-generated, traced or recoloured imagery, no sprite packs. Settings → Art showcase is the living reference. Small sprites (≤ 64 px: items, creatures, hero, emblems, quest-giver faces) are drawn at their designed size and then doubled twice with Scale2x (`hires()` in pixelart.js) for 4× the pixels with the same design; layout and snapping use the logical size (`logicalOf`). Sprite frames may step; motion and particles stay continuous.
- Each skill's quick guide is a pop-up shown once: inside the skill's unlock pop-up when it unlocks, otherwise the first time its page is opened (not inline at the top of the page); closing it in any way counts as seen, and it waits while another dialog is open. Keep routine finds and achievements in the bell. Skill and Hero level-ups and unlocks must be visible even on the current page, with the unlocked content/perks explained. Menu counts reflect current available actions, not unread notifications. Do not add forced tours.
- Preserve existing saves, town restoration, gear comparisons and selling from both inventory and bank.
- Gear arrows (`DQ.gearVerdict`/`gearMark`) add up the stat changes against the equipped item for every slot, weapons included: green when gains outweigh losses, red when not. They appear in the backpack, bank, shop, recipe cards, tooltips and the item panel.
- Equipped copies live outside inventory. Preserve the spare-copy labels, item conservation during swaps/unequips, and the guard against re-equipping an identical non-ammo item.
- Check desktop and narrow-screen layouts and both themes when changing UI.
- Never commit personal game saves, credentials, test sessions or machine-specific configuration.

- Steam on Windows x64 and macOS (Intel/Apple Silicon) with shared Cloud progression is the release target. Preserve the desktop storage bridge and identical save schema/file names; browser storage is a preview fallback. See desktop/STEAM.md before changing persistence or packaging. Never claim Steam Cloud is activated or native builds are validated without the real App ID and platform tests.
- Animations use continuous browser timelines/native refresh rates, with no stepped easing or hard 60 Hz repaint loops. Particle and background motion must use elapsed time so high-refresh screens do not change animation speed.
- Keep skill progress in compact cards above the activity, independent of long choices lists. Combat status/preparation belongs with the fight; loot is a separate lower-priority card. Both themes must use a single non-repeating arena background.

- Living World update (Claude Code, see HANDOFF.md): contextual routes (`js/routes.js`), adventures and discoveries (`js/adventures.js`), restoration voices and regional rewards (`js/journey.js`), procedural ambience (`js/audio.js`), game-feel moments (`js/feel.js`). Extend these instead of adding parallel systems. Adventures are ordinary quests with `secret`/`adventure`/`stage`; discoveries never roll in forecasts and are recorded quietly offline.
- Combat balance evidence: `node scripts/sim-bosses.cjs` (ladder; COUNTER=1, COMPARE=1, PARITY=1) and `node scripts/sim-uniques.cjs`; see `docs/combat-balance.md`. Empty ability slots fill at fight start (Brace first) unless the player has removed an ability themselves (`settings.autoAbil`).
- First hour (docs/first-hour.md, `scripts/play-first-hour.cjs`): Next step never sends a player to train with less food than their health (`DQ.foodStep`), points at a lagging melee skill with a one-click focus switch, and its equip/slot/challenge steps act directly. Combat XP counts damage up to the creature's remaining health; a first boss victory ends the fight.
- Economy changes need evidence: `node scripts/sim-economy.cjs 24` (and `--no-economy` for comparison). See `docs/economy-evidence.md`. Human playtesting follows `docs/playtest-plan.md`.
- Towns to one scale (`js/towns.js`): each town scene is 640 × 360 (doubled with Scale2x, fills the Village panel; phones pan it sideways) with townsfolk ~10 px, homes two to three times that, restoration pieces at 1× (32 px) among the homes and landmarks at 2×. Things you own appear at that scale (boats moored at Saltmere, traps where Beastmastery lures, crops by the cottage); add new owned things through the `extras` argument and the scene cache key in `pixel_runtime.js`. Each activity's backdrop is a close-up district (`districtFor`, 240 × 100) shown at 3× with the 3× hero (72 × 96) and target, feet on the same ground line: any new activity kind or recipe skill needs a district.
